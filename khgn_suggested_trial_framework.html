A trail-marked method for proving a genealogical claim

2. The TrailTest each record as you find it

4. The SummitConfident, but never closed

What This Is, and What It Isn't

This framework marks out the trail a careful researcher follows to prove a genealogical claim, from the first fork in the road to the summit. It is not the Genealogical Proof Standard published by the Board for Certification of Genealogists (BCG). It's something KHGN built on its own terms, drawing what's useful from professional practice and departing from it wherever the departure serves the actual work of genealogical research better.

Two departures are worth naming plainly, up front, rather than leaving you to discover them later:

  • We aim for strong, honestly earned confidence, not legal proof. A family history conclusion isn't held to any legal standard, and the Genealogical Proof Standard doesn't use legal language. What we're after is a conclusion sure-footed enough that another careful researcher, walking the same trail with the same records, would reach the same place.
  • Reaching the summit doesn't end the journey. A record can surface next year that changes everything you thought you knew. This framework builds that openness into the summit itself, rather than treating a proven claim as a locked door.
This is a self-directed practice framework, not coursework. Using it does not constitute formal education, training credit, a teaching credential, or any accredited instruction. It isn't affiliated with, endorsed by, or equivalent to BCG's certification process. It's simply a way of organizing your own thinking when you sit down to research, nothing more formal than that.

This is meant to build the habit of honest, rigorous proof, not to teach source citation formatting, paleography, or jurisdiction-specific record law, and not to stand in for BCG's actual published standard. For the full professional discipline this framework is inspired by, see the Board for Certification of Genealogists.

Stop One: The Fork, Scout Both Paths Before You Choose

Most researchers take the first path that looks right, following a name match or a family story, and only discover the other path later, if at all, usually after they're already attached to being right. This framework asks you to stop at the fork and scout both paths before you commit to either.

Before you decide what you believe, lay out the case for your leading candidate and the case against them, including the strongest version of the case for whoever else could be the right person. This is deliberate, and it's the most important habit the framework builds.

By putting both paths on the map before any commitment is made, this framework tries to catch confirmation bias at the earliest possible point, rather than treating it as something to correct later. If you can't yet describe a real, honest case for the alternative candidate, you're not ready to choose a path. You're ready to go scout one.

What belongs at this stop:

  • The specific research question you're investigating
  • The leading candidate, and the honest case for them
  • The strongest alternative candidate (or explanation), and the honest case for them
  • No conclusion yet, just two paths, described fairly

Stop Two: The Trail, Test Each Record as You Find It

You rarely gather all your supporting records first and only then meet the complications. In real research, a supporting record, a complicating record, and another supporting record arrive in whatever order the archive happens to hand them to you. So this framework treats evidence as one continuous trail, tested step by step.

At this stop, you record each record as you find it, and you let it talk back to you as you go:

  • Landmarks: each record, described plainly: what it says, and where it came from
  • Challenges: for each record, immediately ask what could be wrong with it. A second person of the same name, a wrong trail? A transcription error? A record that only proves something next to what you need it to prove?
  • Weight: not all evidence is equal. A pension file naming specific children carries more weight than a bare tax list with only a name and acreage. Say so, honestly, as you go. Don't save that judgment for later.

The goal of this stop isn't the strongest-sounding claim. It's the most honestly tested one, one step at a time.

Stop Three: The Overlook, Look Back Over Both Paths

Before the final climb, stop at the overlook and look back over both paths one more time. Restate both sides' strongest points, in your own words, before reaching any conclusion.

This is a genuinely harder exercise than it sounds. If you can only summarize your own conclusion and can't fairly restate the best version of the other path, that's a sign you never really engaged with it in the first place. You just noted that it existed and kept walking. A look back that can't do justice to both paths isn't an overlook. It's a highlight reel.

What belongs at this stop:

  • A fair, complete restatement of the case for your conclusion
  • An equally fair, complete restatement of the strongest case against it: not a straw man, the real thing
  • Only after both are stated: your own assessment of which path actually held up better, and why

Stop Four: The Summit, Confident but Never Closed

State your conclusion here: a strong, honestly earned confidence that your claim is true, sure-footed enough to withstand real scrutiny from another careful researcher.

But the summit stays open to new evidence. If a record surfaces, a DNA match complicates the picture, or a document you trusted turns out to be flawed, the trail reopens. This isn't a weakness in your conclusion. It's the honest acknowledgment that historical research is never actually finished, and treating a conclusion as permanently closed is exactly the kind of overconfidence this whole framework exists to prevent.

What belongs at this stop:

  • Your conclusion, stated plainly
  • What specific evidence would be strong enough to reopen the trail if it appeared
  • An explicit acknowledgment that the conclusion stands only until better evidence says otherwise

How This Fits KHGN's Star Ratings

The framework is how a claim makes the climb. Research gathered on the trail, from ★1 Footprint to ★5 Mapped Route, is ready for the framework. When you submit a claim through Build Your Case, you become a Settler bringing a claim, and reviewers follow it up the mountain:

RatingWhat it means
★6 Base CampThe claim is submitted for review
★7 SwitchbackEvery stop is complete, and each record is tied to the fact it proves
★8 Ridge LineScouts confirm that independent records agree
★9 Final AscentEvery challenge has been answered
★10 Claim GrantedProven, and verified by two Surveyors

Who Travels with a Claim

Every claim submitted for review makes the journey with a company of people, each with a role drawn from Kentucky's settlement:

TitleRole
SettlerThe claimant, who submits the claim for review
Caravan LeaderThe claim's guide and advocate, who helps the Settler present the claim at its strongest and most honest, and answer challenges. A Caravan Leader never reviews the same claim.
ScoutsTrail Reviewers, a panel of twelve who examine the evidence and weigh in, each opinion weighted by the evidence behind it
SentinelsChallengers, who keep watch and raise a challenge when new evidence calls a claim into question
SurveyorsProof Reviewers, two experienced researchers who verify the claim against the standards. Both must agree to grant it.
Land CommissionersSecond-review panelists, three reviewers who hear a request for a second review if a Settler believes the standards were misapplied

On the frontier, scouts read the land, sentinels kept watch, and no claim was granted until surveyors had verified and marked it. KHGN's review works the same way: everyone travels toward the truth, and no one person decides alone.

Why Build It This Way

Every choice in this framework serves the same goal: catching a researcher's own wishful thinking as early and as thoroughly as possible, and refusing to let a conclusion harden into unquestionable fact just because it was reached with confidence once.

This is KHGN's own framework, built collaboratively, on our own terms, borrowing what serves the work and leaving behind what doesn't.

This framework was developed by Kentucky History & Genealogy Network, Inc. It is inspired by, but distinct from, the Genealogical Proof Standard published by the Board for Certification of Genealogists. It is intended to build sound research habits, not to serve as a substitute for professional certification standards.
