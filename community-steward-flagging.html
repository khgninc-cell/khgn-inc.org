Level 1 of 3

What to Flag

Flag anything that appears to break KHGN's group rules. You don't need to be certain; that's what moderators are for.

This level is the starting point.

The kinds of posts to flag

  • Modern politics or religious debate, especially support for or opposition to a candidate.
  • Selling, advertising, or paid services, including anything that asks for money within one click of a link.
  • Private information about living people, such as addresses, phone numbers, or birth dates.
  • Copied paid content, like a full article or obituary pasted from a subscription site.
  • Spam and scams: link shorteners, lookalike web addresses, "share before it's deleted" pressure, or posts that seem designed to trick people.
  • Harassment or insults that break the Code of Conduct.
  • Engagement bait, like "type AMEN" or "share if you agree."

When in doubt, flag

You're flagging items that are assumed to be in violation. If something seems off, flag it, and let a trained moderator decide. A flag isn't an accusation; it's a request for a second look. Moderators would much rather review a post that turns out fine than miss one that isn't.

What not to flag: posts you simply disagree with, historical topics that mention elections or churches in a genealogical context, and honest research questions, even if the research seems shaky.

Level 2 of 3

How to Flag

Flag quietly, through the proper tools, and leave the decision to the moderators.

Complete Level 1 to unlock this level.
Community Stewards flag; they never enforce. Don't argue in the comments, call anyone out, or message the person who posted. Report it and let the moderators handle it.

On Facebook

  1. Open the post's menu (the ⋯ at the top right of the post).
  2. Choose the option to report it to the group's admins, not the option to report it to Facebook. Facebook's wording may change over time, but both options are in that menu, so check which one you're choosing.
  3. If asked for a reason, pick the one that fits best, such as spam or a rule violation.

That sends it privately to the group's moderators and administrators for review. The person who posted isn't told who flagged it.

Always the admins, never Facebook

Flagged posts should always go to the group's administrators, not to Facebook. When Facebook has to step in and moderate a group's content itself, it can penalize the whole group. When the group's own administrators handle problems quickly, Facebook sees a safe, well-run group with attentive leadership, which helps the group's standing and reach.

The one exception is a genuine emergency, such as a threat of harm, which should also be reported to the appropriate authorities.

Good flagging habits

  • Don't click suspicious links to check them. Flag the post instead.
  • Don't screenshot and share problem posts elsewhere, even to warn others.
  • Don't flag in bulk or flag someone's posts because of a personal disagreement. Flagging is for rule violations, not settling scores.
  • Serious threats or emergencies, like threats of harm, should also be reported to the appropriate authorities.

On KHGN's own platform

When KHGN launches its own social media platform, Community Stewards will be its flaggers. The flagging button will look different, but everything else in this training stays the same: flag what appears to break the rules, flag quietly, and let moderators decide.

Level 3 of 3

Welcoming and Your Voice

Helping new members feel at home, and helping KHGN see itself as the public does.

Complete Level 2 to unlock this level.

Welcoming new members

  • Greet them warmly when they introduce themselves.
  • Point them to the group rules and free resources, like KYGenWeb.
  • Answer what you can, and invite others to help with what you can't.
  • Model good posts: specific, sourced, and kind.

Never pressure anyone to volunteer, and never ask new members for personal information.

Your voice matters

Community Advisory exists to give KHGN's audience a direct voice, while keeping KHGN a long-standing example of what public service is all about, well into the future. You see KHGN the way the public does, so tell us what's working, what needs changing, and anything confusing or off-putting about how KHGN presents itself. Share suggestions by messaging KHGN's Facebook Page, and describe patterns rather than naming individuals.

Your input shapes recommendations; decisions are made by KHGN's Board. Watch for updates on what changed because of member feedback.
