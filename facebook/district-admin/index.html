Trailhead Training · Level 5 · District Administrator

District Administrator Training

An oversight role across three counties. You don't run a public group; you support and check the County Administrators who do. Four levels, each unlocking the next.

Before you begin

Where this level fits

Prerequisite: County Administrator. Complete Level 4: County Administrator Training and serve as a County Administrator in good standing before starting this level.

4. County AdministratorLeads one county's public group. Three of them report to you monthly.
5. District AdministratorThis level. Oversight of three counties. No public group of your own.
6. Regional AdministratorOversees your district and three others, twelve counties in all, and sits on the Social Media Subcommittee.

A lighter load by design. Your role is oversight, not doing the County Administrators' work for them. Keep your focus on five things: escalations, new issues, spot checks, coverage, and the district report. If a county's volume is consistently too high for one administrator, recommend a co-administrator for that county rather than absorbing the work yourself.

Your progress is saved on this device only, so you can stop and pick up where you left off.

Level 1 of 4

Escalations

Some matters are beyond what a County Administrator should decide alone. Those come to you.

This level is the starting point.

What comes to you

  • Appeals of a County Administrator's own decisions. County Administrators step aside from these, so you review them, or ask another County Administrator in your district to.
  • Disputes involving leadership, such as a moderator or administrator in a conflict with a member or with each other.
  • Problems that cross counties, like a member causing trouble in several groups, or a scam spreading across your district.
  • Requests for help from a County Administrator who's overloaded.

How to handle them

  1. Gather the record: admin notes, the Activity Log, and the County Administrator's account.
  2. Apply the guidelines as written, the same way in all three counties. Consistency across counties is a big part of why your role exists.
  3. Decide, document, and tell the County Administrator why, so they can handle similar cases themselves next time.
  4. Pass up what's beyond you. Matters involving another district, or a dispute involving you, go to your Regional Administrator.

Practice: escalations

Level 2 of 4

New Issues and Temporary Fixes

When County Administrators run into something the guidelines don't cover, they send it to you with a recommendation. You evaluate it and pass it up with your own.

Complete Level 1 to unlock this level.

Reviewing an elevated issue

  1. Confirm it's really new. Sometimes an existing guideline already covers it. If so, point the County Administrator to it, and the issue is resolved.
  2. Check your other counties. Is it happening there too? An issue in all three counties is more urgent than one in a single group.
  3. Add your recommendation to the County Administrator's: agree, adjust, or suggest a different approach, and say why.
  4. Send it to your Regional Administrator, who brings it to the Social Media Subcommittee. If a new guideline is needed, it goes through the Compliance Committee to the Board.
  5. Close the loop with the County Administrator when a decision comes back.

You can also elevate issues you spot yourself across your counties, following the same steps.

Overseeing temporary fixes

County Administrators may put a temporary fix in place while an issue is being decided. Check each one against the limits:

  • Cautious and reversible: hiding rather than deleting, holding rather than approving.
  • Limited to that county's group.
  • Never a permanent ban or a new public rule.
  • Reported and documented.

If a temporary fix goes beyond these limits, direct the County Administrator to adjust it. If the same issue appears in your other counties, you may recommend that they use the same temporary fix, so the district handles it consistently. When a decision comes back, make sure every temporary fix is ended and replaced by the new guidance.

Practice: new issues

Level 3 of 4

Spot Checks and Support

Checking quality without micromanaging, keeping all three counties covered, and developing your County Administrators.

Complete Level 2 to unlock this level.

Spot checks, not full audits

County Administrators audit their moderators; you spot-check your County Administrators. Each month, pick a small sample in each county and look for:

  • Potential Spam decisions: were approvals true false positives?
  • Bans and appeals: documented, consistent with the guidelines, and handled fairly?
  • Moderator audits: is the County Administrator actually checking the Activity Log and applying the discipline policy?
  • Consistency across counties: are similar cases handled the same way in all three?

When you find a problem, start with coaching. Persistent problems follow the same proportional approach as the moderator discipline policy, and serious ones go to your Regional Administrator.

Coverage and vacancies

  • Coordinate coverage when a county is short-handed, for example by arranging help from Field Representatives or, temporarily, from another County Administrator in your district. Coverage is shared across the team, not carried by you alone.
  • Combine the three counties' vacancy lists into a district list. Positions only, never names.
  • Recommend a co-administrator for any county whose volume is consistently more than one administrator can handle.

Developing County Administrators

New County Administrators complete a hands-on period under your guidance. When they're ready, your sign-off completes their appointment and earns them the County Administrator role banner.

Ask for help yourself

If your district's needs outgrow what you can oversee, tell your Regional Administrator early. The same rule you teach your County Administrators applies to you.

Practice: oversight

Level 4 of 4

The District Report

Each month you combine your three counties' reports into one district report for your Regional Administrator. Regional reports roll up into the quarterly report presented to the Board.

Complete Level 3 to unlock this level.
District reports contain numbers and positions only. Never include members' or volunteers' names, profiles, or personal information.

District report builder

Enter each county's numbers from its monthly report. Totals add up automatically. Then add your own notes, and copy or download the finished report for your Regional Administrator. Nothing you enter is saved or sent anywhere by this page.
