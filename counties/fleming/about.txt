Formed | February 10, 1798, the 26th Kentucky county
Formed from | Mason County
Named for | Col. John Fleming, pioneer and Revolutionary War officer
County seat | Flemingsburg (1798 to today)
Later counties | Part of Fleming went to form Floyd (1800) and Rowan (1856)
History | Fleming County lies in northeastern Kentucky, with the Licking River forming its southwestern boundary. It was taken out of Mason County in 1798.
History | Col. John Fleming came to the area in 1776 to mark and improve land. He served as an officer in the Revolutionary War and built Fleming's Station in 1788. His half-brother, George Stockton, owned the land where Flemingsburg stands and laid out and named the town.
History | The first courthouse, likely of logs, was replaced in 1830 and again in 1952. The County Clerk holds marriage, land, and probate records from 1798.
History | In 1998 the Kentucky General Assembly named Fleming County the Covered Bridge Capital of Kentucky. Its three remaining covered bridges are Goddard "White" Bridge over Sand Lick Creek, Ringos Mill Bridge (1867), and Hillsboro, or Grange City, Bridge, both on Fox Creek.
History | Goddard "White" Bridge is the only surviving example of the Ithiel Town lattice design in Kentucky. Its timbers are joined with wooden pegs, and its building date is unknown.
