Title: King News, January 1922
Source: Mountain Advocate (Barbourville, Ky.), January 20, 1922, page 4
Link: https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn87060032/1922-01-20/ed-1/?sp=4&r=0.72,0.693,0.441,0.229,0
Uncle Frank Taylor is still very sick.
Miss Delora Prichard was a guest of Miss Nannie Pope Sunday.
Lee Miracle visited the home of Geo. Mackey in Mackey Bend Sunday.
Miss Ora Shelton spent Saturday and Sunday with home folks.
Little Illena Elliott is on the sick list.
W. R. Vaughn is at King on business this week.
H. M. Prichard has moved to Barbourville.
School is getting along fine with a large attendance.
Will Cobb has moved to the farm that he bought from J. W. Maiden.
James Williams, of Mackey Bend, is very sick at his home.
Rev. H. M. Prichard has moved to his new property in South Barbourville.
John Prichard, of Foley Hollow, has moved into the H. M. Prichard place on Prichard Branch.
W. R. Vaughn and family, of King, have moved to Barbourville and J. W. Maiden and family have moved onto the Vaughn property at King.
Uncle Oliver Partin, of Poplar Creek, died Monday night, Jan. 9. His wife died only a few days before.
Little Viola Hembree has been on the sick list but is better now.
John Logan and family have moved into the Wm. Miller property at this place.
County Agent Earl Mayhew was a visitor at Logan Gap school Tuesday and made an interesting talk on club work.
Cleve Gatliff, who is in the hospital at Harlan, is very much improved and his chance for recovery is very bright. It has not yet been learned who shot him.
Joe Ferguson says he thinks Abe Lincoln was a great man, that's why he wants to stay 16th in his spelling class so he can always be "Abe Lincoln."
The King Telephone people have been repairing their telephone line. Let's hope that the Farmer Telephone people will do likewise.
Earl Mays, of Clate, was at Logan Gap Tuesday.
Maud Mackey of Mackey Bend, was here Friday.
John Farmer, of Prichard Branch, went to Anchor Monday on business.
Messrs. James Shelton, Caleb Runyon, Chester Fusan and L. E. Miracle, all of Poplar Creek, attended the singing at Mackey Bend Sunday.
A Farmer's Boy.
