# Rockcastle County at a Glance — about.txt for counties/rockcastle/
# Drafted with the help of AI, with human review. Check facts before publishing.
# Note: sources give different exact dates in 1810 (January 8, January 18,
# April 1), so only the year is used. The state marker calls it the 52nd county.
Formed | 1810, the 52nd Kentucky county
Formed from | Knox, Lincoln, Madison, and Pulaski counties
Named for | The Rockcastle River, named for a lone rock that looked like an ancient castle
County seat | Mount Vernon (1810 to today)
Later counties | Part of Rockcastle went to form Laurel (1826) and Jackson (1858)
Records note | The courthouse burned in 1873, destroying most early county records
History | Isaac Lindsey, who came through with a hunting party in 1767, saw a lone rock that resembled an ancient castle. The river was named for it, and later the county. Dr. Thomas Walker's party passed through the area in 1750.
History | Two great pioneer roads, the Wilderness Road and Skaggs' Trace, crossed the county, carrying hundreds of thousands of settlers into Kentucky in the late 1700s and early 1800s. Mount Vernon grew from settlements around Langford's Station, established about 1792.
History | Great Saltpetre Cave supplied saltpeter for gunpowder during the War of 1812. The cave is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
History | In 1939 John Lair began the Renfro Valley Barn Dance, broadcast on radio from the valley just north of Mount Vernon. Renfro Valley has drawn visitors to the county ever since.
History | For researchers: the 1873 courthouse fire destroyed nearly all local marriage, estate, court, and land records. Check state records, census records, church and cemetery records, and records of the parent counties: Knox, Lincoln, Madison, and Pulaski.
