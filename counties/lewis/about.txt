Formed | December 2, 1806, the 48th Kentucky county
Formed from | Mason County
Named for | Meriwether Lewis of the Lewis and Clark Expedition
County seat history | Poplar Flat (1806), Clarksburg (1809), Vanceburg (1863–64 to today)
History | Lewis County lies in northeastern Kentucky along the Ohio River, with Adams and Scioto counties, Ohio, across the water. Its Ohio River border is the longest of any Kentucky county.
History | The county was taken from Mason County in 1806. Its first court met at the log home of Oke Hendrickson at Poplar Flat. The seat moved to Clarksburg in 1809, then to Vanceburg during the Civil War years, 1863–64.
History | Vanceburg was laid out in 1797 and named for Joseph Vance. It was sometimes called "Alum City" for the alum deposits nearby.
History | Kentucky was the first state to name a county for Meriwether Lewis. William Clark has no Kentucky county of his own, because Clark County already honored his brother, George Rogers Clark.
History | Lewis County leaned strongly Union during the Civil War. In 1884 its citizens raised the Union Monument in Vanceburg, honoring the 107 Lewis County soldiers who died for the Union. The state historical marker calls it the only Union monument south of the Mason-Dixon line put up by public subscription outside a cemetery.
History | Early settlements included Salt Lick, Kinniconick, Quicks Run, Cabin Creek, Sycamore, Poplar Flat, Concord, Tollesboro, Vanceburg, Clarksburg, Quincy, and Petersville. In 1904 part of Lewis County briefly went into the short-lived Beckham County.
