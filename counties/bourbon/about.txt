Formed | 1785, by the Virginia legislature, before Kentucky's statehood
Formed from | Fayette County
Named for | The French royal House of Bourbon, honoring France's aid during the American Revolution
History | Bourbon County is one of the counties Virginia created in its Kentucky district before statehood. Formed in 1785 from Fayette County, the original Bourbon County covered a vast area of northern and eastern Kentucky, sometimes called "Old Bourbon."
History | Its county seat, Paris, was first known as Hopewell, then Bourbonton, before taking the name Paris, another tribute to France.
History | Because Old Bourbon was so large, many later counties were formed wholly or partly from its territory. Researchers tracing early families anywhere in northern and eastern Kentucky may find them in Bourbon County's early records, and records from before 1785 are found in Fayette County.
History | The county's name is also linked in popular history to bourbon whiskey, though historians still debate exactly how the spirit got its name.
Source | John E. Kleber, ed., The Kentucky Encyclopedia (Lexington: University Press of Kentucky, 1992)
