Working With AI Responsibly

AI tools can be a genuine help in genealogy, and a fast way to spread errors. In four short levels, learn where AI helps, where it misleads, how to handle AI-altered images, and why the human touch decides. KHGN's principle is simple: AI assists. People verify.

Level 1 of 4

Where AI Helps

Used well, AI is like a tireless research assistant: quick, helpful, and always in need of checking.

Good jobs for AI

  • Reading difficult handwriting: AI can offer a first reading of a hard-to-read will or letter. You then check every word against the image yourself, especially names, dates, and numbers.
  • Translating: a German church record, a Latin baptism, or a French land grant. Treat the translation as a draft and confirm key details.
  • Planning research: asking what kinds of records might exist for an 1840s Kentucky marriage, or which repositories hold a county's deeds.
  • Organizing: sorting notes into a timeline, building a research checklist, or formatting a citation you've already gathered the details for.

The rule for every AI answer

Whatever AI tells you about an ancestor is a ★1 Footprint, a lead just like a family story. It can point you toward a record, but it proves nothing until you find that record and read it yourself.

Level 2 of 4

Where AI Misleads

AI writes fluently and confidently, even when it's wrong. Knowing its common mistakes is your best protection.

Four common traps

  • Invented sources: AI can produce citations that look completely real, with page numbers, archive names, and collection titles, for records that don't exist. Always follow a citation to the actual record.
  • Merged identities: two men with the same name in the same county can be blended into one person, the classic "Same Name, Wrong Man" error, delivered with confidence.
  • Filled gaps: when records are missing, AI may supply a plausible guess, such as a birth year, a parent, or a migration route, and present it as fact.
  • Repeated errors: AI learns from what's already online, including copied, unsourced trees, so it can repeat a popular mistake as if it were settled.

A quick test for any AI claim

Ask: What record says so, and can I see it? If the answer is no, or the record can't be found, the claim stays a lead.

Level 3 of 4

Images and AI

AI can sharpen, restore, colorize, and even invent images. Some of that is delightful, but none of it is evidence.

How AI changes images

  • Enhancement and restoration can change faces, smooth away details, or alter the letters and numbers on a document.
  • Colorization guesses at colors that were never recorded.
  • Generated "ancestor portraits" show people who never looked that way, sometimes presented as real photographs.

The rules for images

  • Only an unaltered image counts as evidence. A faithful scan or photograph of a record can be physical evidence; an enhanced version can't.
  • Keep the original file whenever you restore or enhance a photo.
  • Label altered images clearly, such as "AI-colorized" or "AI-generated illustration," so no one mistakes them for originals.
  • Never present a generated portrait as a real ancestor.

Level 4 of 4

The Human Touch

AI can suggest. People decide. Here's what only you can do, and how to use AI honestly.

What only people can do

  • Confirm the record exists and actually says what's claimed.
  • Weigh the witness: who gave the information, and how would they know?
  • Rule out the wrong trails that separate people who share a name.
  • Reach a conclusion that honestly admits what remains uncertain.

That's why, at KHGN, ratings are decided by people on the trail: Scouts (Trail Reviewers) who examine the evidence, and two Surveyors (Proof Reviewers) who verify it, not an AI.

Use AI honestly

Disclose when AI helped, such as "handwriting transcribed with AI help and checked against the original." Using AI openly isn't a problem; hiding it is. Disclosure tells others which parts to double-check.

Protect privacy

Many AI services keep what you type, and some may use it for other purposes. Don't paste living people's names, addresses, or birth dates, private family records, or anything from KHGN's private training into AI tools. The same standards you learned in the Tipple Trial apply.
